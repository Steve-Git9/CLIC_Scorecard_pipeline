## Executive summary

The TIDEMESH project develops and validates adaptive sensor networks that help operators to monitor the condition of coastal infrastructure and to plan maintenance before problems become serious. Five organisations from industry and research combine their strengths. The consortium seeks support from Business Finland.

The work is organised in 6 work packages over 30 months. The requested funding is detailed in section 5.3.

## Project volume

Total project volume including in kind contributions: EUR 2,003,400

Scaling from a trial to routine operation brings challenges that are different from those of development. Larger installations demand reliable logistics, clear ownership of maintenance tasks and dependable supply of components. The consortium collects experience on these topics during the trials and records it in guidance for future deployments. This knowledge is as valuable as the technology itself.

Open science practices are followed wherever they are compatible with commercial interests. Publications are made accessible, and datasets that are not sensitive are shared with suitable descriptions. Software components that have little commercial value are released so that others can build on them. The partners agree in advance which results are suitable for open release.

## Key point

Good collaboration depends on clear roles, open communication and a willingness to adapt plans when evidence points in a new direction.

The commercial perspective is considered from the earliest stage of the work. Questions about pricing, support, installation and maintenance influence technical choices. Potential customers are asked what they would need in order to trust and purchase the solution. Their answers shape the product concept and the evidence that the partners must collect during the trials. 1

The final phase of the project concentrates on consolidating results and preparing for the period after funding. Remaining gaps are closed, documentation is completed and agreements for continued cooperation are discussed. The partners also prepare a plan for maintaining key outputs such as software, datasets and test facilities, so that the investment continues to deliver value.

Coastal communities depend on infrastructure that is exposed to changing conditions throughout the year. Monitoring approaches that were designed for stable environments often struggle to keep pace with this variability. The project responds by combining flexible sensing with analysis that adapts to local circumstances. This allows operators to understand the state of their assets earlier and to plan maintenance in a way that reduces disruption for the people who rely on them every day.

Interoperability with existing systems is a decisive factor for adoption. Operators have invested in equipment and processes that they cannot replace quickly. The solution is therefore designed to connect to common interfaces and to coexist with what is already in place. Migration paths are described so that users can adopt new functions step by step and keep their operations running. 2

Regional and local actors are important partners in the wider effort even where they do not take part formally. Public authorities, operators and community groups hold knowledge about local conditions and priorities. Dialogue with them increases the relevance of the work and prepares the ground for later adoption. The consortium keeps a record of these contacts and the advice received.

## Good practice

Simple interfaces between components make it easier to exchange parts, to test in isolation and to involve new partners later.

Monitoring of progress relies on a modest set of indicators that are easy to collect and hard to misinterpret. Each work package reports on completion of tasks, quality of results and use of resources. Deviations are discussed openly and lead to corrective action when needed. The aim is to detect problems early and to avoid reports that describe activity without showing real advancement.

Evaluation of the results uses criteria that were defined together with the intended users. Technical performance is only a part of the picture; usability, cost of ownership and fit with existing practices matter just as much. Evidence is collected in a consistent way across the trial sites so that findings can be compared. The evaluation report states clearly where the solution meets expectations and where it does not. 3

Training and knowledge transfer are built into the work plan. Early career researchers and engineers take part in project activities and gain experience that is valuable for their later careers. Material prepared for internal use is reviewed so that it can be offered to the wider community. The aim is to leave behind capabilities that persist after the funding has ended.

User involvement takes several forms, from interviews and observation to hands on testing of prototypes. Participants are chosen to reflect the range of conditions in which the solution will be used. Their feedback is recorded systematically and discussed by the whole consortium. This ensures that design decisions are based on evidence of how people work and not only on the preferences of the developers.

Testing in realistic environments is essential for solutions that must perform outdoors. Laboratory results give valuable insight but cannot reproduce the full complexity of weather, wear and human behaviour. Field trials are therefore planned with care, including preparation, safety measures and procedures for recording observations. Lessons from the trials flow back into design and are documented for later users. 4

## 1 Challengeandsolution

## 1.1 Context and challenge

Data collected during the project will be handled according to principles of responsibility and transparency. Each data source is described together with its origin, its quality and the conditions under which it may be shared. Access is granted on the basis of need and recorded in a way that can be audited. Where data relate to commercially sensitive operations, the partners agree on handling rules before any exchange takes place.

Exploitation of results is the main motivation for the industrial partners. They contribute market knowledge from the start and test early versions against the requirements of paying customers. Research partners contribute methods and evidence that give the resulting products credibility. Together the partners prepare routes to market that do not depend on a narrow customer base or on a narrow application.

## In brief

Open and honest reporting of difficulties allows the consortium to react in time and is regarded as a sign of professional maturity.
